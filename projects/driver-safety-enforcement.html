Driver safety enforcement

Read all the rider complaints on the same day and in all the languages. Then apply a consequence that agrees with the severity. Small cases are automatic, and a person decides the serious cases.

Namma Yatri · Central data team · 2024–2025 · Owner: classifier, scoring and action logic

This is an industry project. The code and the data are proprietary, thus this page shows only the method.

Problem

Riders give feedback after each ride. The feedback is a rating with an optional comment. Riders also open support tickets in the application or with the call centre. Most of the feedback is about fares, routes, and cancellations. A small part describes an unsafe condition: dangerous driving, harassment, alcohol, or threats. On a platform where the safety of the rider is the main promise of the product, this small part is very important.

Before

Support agents read the tickets one at a time and sent the serious tickets to a specialist. Nobody could see all the history of one driver. A group of complaints about one driver thus looked like separate events, and the serious cases became visible some days late.

Goal

Read all the feedback on the same day. Put the safety complaints in the order of their severity. Then give the driver a consequence that agrees with the severity and that is the same for all drivers. Small cases are automatic, and a person decides the serious cases.

The text was the difficult part. The feedback came in Kannada, Hindi, and English. Riders also wrote Kannada and Hindi with the Latin alphabet, and the spelling is different for each rider. Rules with keywords and models for one language thus both fail.

Data

Unit
One item of feedback, which is a rating comment or a support ticket. Each item has a link to a ride, a driver, and a rider.
Languages
Kannada, Hindi, English, and mixtures with the Latin alphabet. The Sarvam AI translation API, which is made for Indian languages, translated each item to English before the classification. The classifier thus learns only English patterns.
Labels
Five severity groups, and also a group for the items that are not safety complaints. The support team labelled the first set of items with a written guide. After the release, each case that the operations team examined became a new labelled item.
Imbalance
The safety items are a small part of all the feedback, and the most severe groups are very rare. This condition controls the choices of the model and of the thresholds below.

Approach

  1. Translate first, then classify

    The Sarvam translation API changed each comment into English. It also translated the Kannada and the Hindi that riders wrote with the Latin alphabet, which is a problem for most models. The multilingual difficulty is thus outside the model, and a simple classifier that a person can examine was sufficient. The classifier uses TF-IDF (term frequency, inverse document frequency) features on word n-grams and character n-grams. An n-gram is a short group of words or letters, and it tolerates the spelling errors that stay after the translation. A linear classifier then calculates the group. The operations team could see which words caused a flag.

  2. Threshold for recall at the top

    The costs of the two errors are very different. A missed report of an assault is much worse than one more case for a person to examine. The thresholds of the severe groups thus give a high recall: the system finds as many true cases as possible and accepts some false alarms. The review step then removes the false alarms.

  3. Severity chooses the lever, the window scales with it

    The system collects the flags of each driver for each group. Groups 1 to 3 cause a block, and the length of the block increases with the severity. The block is permanent for the highest group and a fixed number of days for the lower groups. Groups 4 and 5 cause a lower priority: the driver stays online, but the ride matching puts the driver after the other drivers for some time, and the system gives the reason. The loss of rides for one week is the warning. The matching service already had priority levels, but this was the first heavy use of that function. The enforcement thus became a graded cost and not a switch with two positions.

  4. Humans on every block

    The lower priority is automatic. Each block goes first to a queue. The queue shows the original text, the translation, the history of the driver, and the recommended action. The driver operations team then confirms or refuses the action with one click. A refused case goes back as a corrected label. A driver can also make an appeal to driver support.

  5. Daily batch, not real time

    A person examines the feedback on the same day, and this speed is sufficient for the problem. A streaming system adds complexity but gives no better result.

The comparison of the complaints is a comparison of two periods, thus it contains all the other changes of the period. Also, most drivers with one complaint never get a second complaint, thus a part of the result occurs with each policy. The number of approximately 12,000 drivers is therefore a count of the drivers that received the lighter lever, and not a measurement of a change of behaviour.

Key insight Most drivers with a complaint are not dangerous. They had a bad day, or they had a habit that nobody told them about. A system with only a warning and a block is too weak for these cases, or too strong. The priority in the ride matching gives a third option. The cost agrees with the problem, the driver feels it immediately in the earnings, and good behaviour removes it. Most of the approximately 12,000 drivers in this group got no new complaint, and a person examined the evidence before each of the approximately 550 blocks.

What went wrong, and what changed

Safety as a fare complaint

Some riders used the safety category for a dispute about the fare, because this category gets attention. A small number of riders also used it after an argument. The step with a person exists mainly for this reason.

Vocabulary drift

A change of the fares caused many complaints with new words that the classifier did not know. The correction is a weekly examination of a sample of the feedback that the classifier did not flag. This sample finds the missed cases and not the false alarms.

Early pushback

In the first weeks, the driver operations team did not agree with some blocks. This caused the rule that no block occurs without a confirmation from a person. The rule made the system sufficiently reliable to keep.

What I'd do differently

Measure the lever

Change the length of the lower priority at random for the drivers in group 5. The data then shows how much cost is necessary to change the behaviour, and the length is not only a decision of a person.

Rider credibility

Use the complaint history of the rider as a feature. A first complaint from a frequent rider and a fifth complaint from a rider with a dispute about a fare are not the same evidence.

Held-out evaluation

Make a fixed set of labelled items to measure the precision and the recall of each group with time. Also record the number of blocks that a person refuses in the queue. This number is a quality signal that the system already gives every day.

Related work

A graded consequence is a usual method in the trust and safety teams of marketplaces, and the Santa Clara Principles give the usual expectations for notice and appeal. Two parts of this project are different. First, the consequence uses the priority levels of the matching service. Second, a translation step removes the multilingual problem, thus the classifier stays simple. The models of Sarvam AI are made for Indic languages, and they made the translation sufficiently reliable.

Code & artifacts

The code is proprietary to Namma Yatri. Stack: Python with scikit-learn (TF-IDF and a linear classifier) and pandas, the Sarvam AI translation API, SQL on the ride data and the feedback data, a daily scheduled job, and the block queue and the priority controls of the matching service.
