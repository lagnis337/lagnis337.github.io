Voice AI support agent

Answer the driver support calls that repeat every day, in whichever language the driver speaks, and pass the rest to a person.

Namma Yatri · Central data team · 2024–2025 · Owner, end to end

Industry project. The code and data are proprietary, so this page describes the method only.

Problem

Drivers called support with the same handful of problems every day, and each call took 15 to 20 minutes of an agent's time. The agents gave the same answers over and over. That is a poor use of a support team, and drivers waited in a queue for it.

Approach

Two models doing two different jobs, because neither is enough on its own.

How to speak

Gemini Live holds the conversation. It replies in under a second and it sounds like a person. Latency matters more than it sounds: a driver hangs up on a call that pauses.

What to say

The Sarvam models are trained on Indian regional languages, and they beat a general model on the words a driver actually uses, in Hindi and Kannada as much as in English.

The agent works in Hindi, English and Kannada. When it cannot solve the problem, it hands the call to a person.

How one call works

  1. The driver speaks, and the agent listens while the driver speaks

    Audio streams to the model as the driver talks. The agent does not wait for the end of the sentence, because a pause longer than a second loses the call.

  2. Speech becomes words

    The recognition layer turns the audio into text, in Hindi, English or Kannada. It is the weakest link in the chain and where most failures begin (see below).

  3. The agent looks up that driver

    It has read access to the data about whoever is calling, so it answers from that driver's actual state rather than from a generic script. The scope of that access is deliberately narrow. We looked at which fields the answers really turned on and gave the agent those, because fetching everything costs time, and time is the one thing a voice call cannot spend.

  4. The words become an intent

    The repeated problems live in a written catalogue of intents, and the agent matches each request to one of them. The Sarvam models do that matching, because they know the vocabulary a driver uses in an Indian language.

  5. The agent answers, or it stops

    On a match, the agent gives that intent's answer and Gemini Live speaks it. It stops and routes the call to the human queue on no match, when the driver asks for a person, when the recognition layer is unsure of what it heard, when the same question fails twice, and on anything touching an accident or a safety complaint.

  6. A person receives the call with the context

    The support agent picks up with the conversation so far in front of them, so the driver does not explain the problem twice.

How we knew it was working

Resolution time and NPS are outcome numbers. Neither tells you whether the agent gave the right answer, so quality was checked by hand. The customer success team and I picked calls every day and listened to them, which is how most of the speech-recognition failures surfaced.

The second source was the drivers. Namma Yatri runs an open house every Wednesday, where any driver can come in and talk to the team. Complaints about the agent came up there, in a setting where a driver will say what a survey never captures.

Results

Resolution time fell by about 75%, comparing the period with the agent against the period before it, when support agents handled the same tickets. Driver NPS (Net Promoter Score) rose 18 points over the same span.

Key insight The generative models were not the hard part. Gemini Live and the Sarvam models both held up; the speech recognition in front of them did not. A voice agent is a chain, and it is worth no more than its first link. Hear the wrong words and everything after is wrong, while the driver repeats a sentence to a machine that cannot follow it.

What went wrong

The hard part was not the part that looks hard. Gemini Live and the Sarvam models were both strong. Speech recognition was not. The models that handle Indian regional speech lagged the rest of the pipeline, and most failures started there. Hear the wrong words and everything downstream is wrong, while the driver repeats a sentence to a machine that cannot follow it.

What I'd do differently

Improve the recognition layer

Speech recognition is the weakest link and the source of most failures. A model trained on these speech patterns, or one tuned on the calls themselves, would remove more errors than any change to the generative layers.

A controlled test

Give the agent to a random share of drivers and keep the rest on the human queue. That separates the agent's effect from everything else that moved in the period, for NPS as well as resolution time. The daily call reviews caught failures, but they could not tell us what the same drivers would have done without the agent.

Related work

The conversation layer uses the Gemini Live API, which Google built for low-latency speech-to-speech. The language layer uses models from Sarvam AI, trained on Indian languages. The design is a division of labour between the two.

Code & artifacts

Proprietary to Namma Yatri. Stack: the Gemini Live API for conversation and speech, the Sarvam models for Indian languages, intent definitions covering the repeated support problems, and a handoff into the human support queue.
