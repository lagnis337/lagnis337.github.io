Driver churn prediction

Every morning, the model puts approximately 150,000 drivers in the order of the risk that they stop to drive. The operations team then acts on this list.

Namma Yatri · Central data team · 2024–2025 · Owner, end to end

This is an industry project. The code and the data are proprietary, thus this page shows only the method.

~150kdrivers scored every morning
XGB + RFXGBoost and random forest, averaged & calibrated, 36 features
AUC ~0.80ranks a driver who will churn above one who won't about 80% of the time (held-out month)
−30 ppchurn among called drivers against a randomised holdout

Problem

Namma Yatri is a ride-hailing platform with no commission. The drivers pay a subscription and not a part of each fare. Driver retention is thus the supply of the marketplace and also a direct source of revenue. Approximately 42% of the active drivers stopped to drive. There is no cancellation event in this business, thus the data has no direct label. A driver is a churned driver when the driver completes no ride in 30 days.

Before

There was no systematic selection. The operations team looked at the drivers with no activity for more than one week and called them. Many of these drivers had already left the platform.

Goal

Calculate each day the probability that a driver stops in the next 30 days. Then give the operations team and the customer success team a list in the order of this probability, with a length that agrees with their capacity.

Data

Unit
One row for each driver and each day. The model calculates a score for approximately 150,000 active drivers each morning. Drivers with a very short time on the platform are not in the training data.
Label
churn_30d = 1 when the driver completes no ride in the 30 days after the score date. The label is available only one month later. The most recent month thus has no label, and the measurement of the effect uses cohorts and not the predictions of the model.
Features
There are 36 features. Most of them use weekly and monthly windows, and all of them use only data from before the score date. The structure is the RFM framework (recency, frequency, monetary value), with feedback data and context data.
Leakage
The recency features are the days from the last trip and the time from the last activity. These features contain the label if you calculate them after the score date. Each window thus stops at the score date.
FamilyFeatures
RecencyDays from the last completed ride. Recent activity against the usual level of the same driver. A busy driver that becomes slow is not the same as a quiet driver that stays quiet.
FrequencyRides completed, days with activity, hours online. The demand funnel: requests, accepted requests, completed rides, and the cancellation rate. The part of the rides that occur at night.
MonetaryAverage fare, average trip distance, and earnings for each hour online. Subscription status, with the days that stay on the plan.
FeedbackRider ratings and complaints
ContextCity and geo-zone. Time on the platform. Demand in the zone, which is the number of requests for each online driver, because a driver needs work in the area. A flag that shows if the operations team called the driver recently.

Approach

  1. Two models, one score

    XGBoost (gradient-boosted decision trees) is the better of the two models. It finds combinations, for example many hours online and a decrease of the acceptance rate. The random forest (scikit-learn) is weaker, but it makes different errors. The final score is the average of the two probabilities, with a calibration that makes the score equal to a true probability. The average was more stable from month to month than each model alone. For a list that the operations team uses each day, this stability is more important than a small increase of the AUC.

  2. Split by time, never at random

    The training data contains the first months and the validation data contains a later month. Churn changes with the incentive programmes and with the season. A random split is not correct, because rows of the same driver go to the training set and to the test set. The training occurred each month on a moving window, when the labels of the month became available.

  3. Decision rule sized to capacity

    The drivers at the top of the list, approximately 100 drivers each day, get a call from the operations team. The next group gets an incentive in coins in the application. The limits agree with the capacity of the teams and not with a probability threshold. A nightly Airflow job writes the list to a shared sheet, and the product team and the customer success team use this sheet.

  4. Close the loop

    The operations team records the reason that each driver gives during the call. These categories go to the product team. A feature that shows a recent call also goes back to the model.

A randomised holdout measured the calls. Approximately 20% of the high-risk drivers were held out and got no call. The drivers who got a call churned 30 percentage points less than the holdout. The change from 42% to 30% is wider than this. It compares two periods for all the drivers, and it includes the product changes that the next paragraph describes, because those reached the holdout as well.

Key insight Drivers who work in the evening want the last ride of the day to end near their home. They refused rides in the other direction, or they went offline. This caused the Go Home function. When a driver starts this function, the driver receives only the requests with a destination near the home. Other reasons from the calls caused changes to the prices and to the match between a driver and a rider. The model selected the drivers to call, the calls changed the product, and the product decreased the churn.

What I'd do differently

Measure the product changes

The holdout separates the calls, but the changes that the calls caused reached every driver, including the holdout. A staggered release by city would measure the Go Home function and the price changes on their own.

Uplift, not risk

Uplift modelling calculates which drivers change their behaviour after a call, and not which drivers have a high risk. The approximately 100 calls each day can then go to the drivers that a call can keep.

Time-to-churn

A survival model calculates when an event occurs. It thus uses the time when a driver stops, and not only the fact that the driver crossed a limit of 30 days.

Drift monitoring

Examine the most important features each week. A change of an incentive programme then becomes visible in the metrics of the model in the same week.

Related work

The frame of the project comes from Tran (2021), which uses the RFM framework for non-contractual churn of riders, on a public Uber data set of approximately 1,400 users (ICABDE 2021). This project uses the same frame for the supply side, because the churn of a driver removes capacity from the marketplace. It also operates in production, with approximately 150,000 drivers each day and with an action loop.

Code & artifacts

The code is proprietary to Namma Yatri. Stack: an SQL feature build that Apache Airflow starts each night, Python with pandas, XGBoost, and scikit-learn, and a daily delivery of the scores to a shared sheet.
